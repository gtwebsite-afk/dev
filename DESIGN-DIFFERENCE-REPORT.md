# Design Difference Report

This report records the creative direction and the deliberate separation between the five new website universes. They are not one template with five palettes: each project has a different content rhythm, information architecture, header, hero grammar, component vocabulary, and mobile priority.

## Automotive — KYNTRA

- **Concept:** Engineered electric performance presented as a technical product instrument.
- **Palette:** Carbon, graphite, cold metal, mist, and a signal orange. No gold, resort green, consulting green, or technology blue/cyan identity.
- **Typography:** Space Grotesk for display/UI with mono-like numeric treatment for specifications.
- **Grid:** Mechanical 12-column field with hairlines, specification rails, and hard clipping.
- **Navigation:** Sticky instrument bar with model/service pathways and a persistent test-drive action.
- **Hero:** Full viewport product reveal: designation, performance metrics, and direct action sit inside the machine-like composition rather than a generic split hero.
- **Shape language:** 0–4px radius, sharp polygons, technical rules, asymmetric cuts.
- **Components:** Spec table, model switcher, comparison matrix, configurator output, service timeline.
- **Motion:** Fast directional wipes and controlled number/spec transitions; reduced motion removes transforms.
- **Mobile strategy:** Swipeable model rail and sticky test-drive action keep performance content task-first.
- **Difference from legacy references:** It is an automotive product instrument, not procurement, software, architecture, consulting, hospitality, healthcare, legal, education, or culinary work; its geometry and signal-orange metal palette avoid the legacy dark-gold card shell.
- **Difference from the other new sites:** It is the only technical, metric-led interface with mechanical clipping and a specification-first content rhythm.

## Fashion — ORVYN

- **Concept:** Directional fashion label combining campaign image crops with a working editorial shop.
- **Palette:** Optical white, near black, chrome gray, and one electric accent; no cream/beige luxury or culinary burgundy.
- **Typography:** Oversized grotesk/neo-grotesk display with a clean commerce reading face; no classical luxury serif.
- **Grid:** Irregular edge-to-edge product and campaign grid with intentional crop changes.
- **Navigation:** Retail taxonomy (new, women, men, objects, editorial, search, bag), designed around browsing rather than corporate sections.
- **Hero:** Campaign crop and oversized type form a visual statement; commerce entry points are embedded in the image/editorial sequence.
- **Shape language:** Hard edges, crop masks, labels, and editorial whitespace; content is not wrapped in uniform cards.
- **Components:** Product gallery, size/variant controls, stock state, wishlist, local bag, lookbook and material notes.
- **Motion:** Image swaps, clip reveals, type crops; reduced motion keeps image state changes instantaneous.
- **Mobile strategy:** Fast shop/filter/bag access and a single-column editorial-to-product flow.
- **Difference from legacy references:** It is fashion commerce, not architecture/interior or hospitality; its optical contrast and retail information architecture reject warm paper, serif portfolio, and resort composition.
- **Difference from the other new sites:** It is the only site where product browsing, garment variants, and editorial campaign crops are the primary interaction.

## Publication — Kabararus

- **Concept:** Independent newsroom with a front-page publishing hierarchy and article reading tools.
- **Palette:** Newsprint white, ink black, cool paper gray, and a restrained signal accent.
- **Typography:** Separate headline face, reading serif, UI sans, and mono timestamp/data roles.
- **Grid:** Dense editorial columns, rules, headline lists, and section bands rather than marketing cards.
- **Navigation:** Utility strip → masthead → topic navigation → front-page content; this is a publishing masthead, not a corporate navbar.
- **Hero:** Lead story and secondary leads establish a front page; there is no generic headline/paragraph/button/image hero.
- **Shape language:** Rules, columns, labels, pull quotes, and text hierarchy; almost no rounded surfaces.
- **Components:** Byline/time/reading time, progress indicator, pull quote, caption, related coverage, author module, archive/search.
- **Motion:** Minimal and subordinate to information density; reduced motion leaves the reading experience unchanged.
- **Mobile strategy:** Section drawer and ordered headline density preserve the newsroom hierarchy on narrow screens.
- **Difference from legacy references:** It is independent journalism, not legal/editorial professional services or technology; its newsprint hierarchy is distinct from ivory institutional composition.
- **Difference from the other new sites:** It is the only information-dense reading product, optimized for scanning, chronology, and article completion.

## Festival — KILATARA

- **Concept:** Live music event system shaped by gig posters, wristbands, stage signage, and wayfinding.
- **Palette:** Near black with acid yellow, electric cyan, and ultraviolet/magenta accents; it avoids the academy cobalt/lime/coral combination.
- **Typography:** Compressed display lettering, giant event numerals, clear UI copy, and optional mono schedule labels.
- **Grid:** Poster panels, ticket cutlines, stage/time markings, and timeline rows.
- **Navigation:** Event-first routes for lineup, schedule, stages, tickets, venue, access, and festival guide.
- **Hero:** In the first viewport it answers what, when, where, and tickets through an event poster composition.
- **Shape language:** Hard edges, cutlines, tape-like labels, and stage blocks; no rounded SaaS card system.
- **Components:** Day selector, stage filter, timeline schedule, favorites, venue map, ticket pathway, accessibility and sustainability notes.
- **Motion:** Kinetic type, ticker, poster transitions, and controlled distortion; all simplified by reduced-motion CSS.
- **Mobile strategy:** Schedule and ticket priority, with compact day/stage controls and saved favorites.
- **Difference from legacy references:** It is a live cultural event, not an academy or corporate service. The poster system, ultraviolet/cyan signal colors, and timeline interaction are outside the legacy visual space.
- **Difference from the other new sites:** It is the only time-based, place-based experience organized around a live schedule.

## Impact — RUTE BAIK

- **Concept:** Evidence-led environmental and social foundation connecting field reporting, geography, programs, and transparent finances.
- **Palette:** Mineral white, dark earth, vivid sky, safety orange, and one ecological accent; no cream/forest resort or consulting-green identity.
- **Typography:** Strong contemporary sans with utility/slab numerals for impact data.
- **Grid:** Map, metric, field-note, and report modules arranged as an evidence trail rather than a corporate service grid.
- **Navigation:** Mission → programs → regions → impact → stories/research → transparency → take action.
- **Hero:** Current mission and live impact evidence lead into geography, not a decorative landscape image.
- **Shape language:** Document tabs, map markers, data bars, report sheets, and practical square controls.
- **Components:** Interactive-looking map, region selector, impact metrics, field-note timeline, report viewer, transparent financial visualization, donation demo.
- **Motion:** Calm map/metric transitions and progressive disclosure; reduced motion disables movement while retaining state changes.
- **Mobile strategy:** Map/program selector and take-action access remain reachable without a heavy global drawer.
- **Difference from legacy references:** It is a field organization built around evidence and geography, not consulting, resort, or wellness. Its documentary/data language rejects luxury hospitality styling.
- **Difference from the other new sites:** It is the only site where program geography, impact evidence, and financial transparency are the main navigation spine.

## Cross-site gate

The five projects intentionally differ across industry, palette, type roles, header hierarchy, hero grammar, grid, shape, CTA, image treatment, motion, mobile priority, and footer function. A similarity check should judge the projects as separate universes rather than recolored variants.
